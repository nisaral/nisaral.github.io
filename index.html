About me

Hi, I’m Keyush Nisar, a researcher and engineer working on uncertainty in learned systems, black-box LLM serving, and runtime evidence for deployed AI. I am pursuing a B.Tech in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning at D.J. Sanghvi College of Engineering, University of Mumbai (2023–2027). I have worked with RAI Tracker in London, CoE-CNDS Labs at VJTI, CerebralZip (a VJTI-incubated startup), and EnsanAI, moving between research questions and production constraints. I care about a simple test: whether a score is good enough to rank alternatives, or whether it is honest enough to be treated as a decision. Research

Independent research — Uncertainty-preserving distillation for diffusion world models 2026 – Present

PyTorch · diffusion models · MBPO / SAC · GitHub

  • Asked whether compressing a diffusion world model into a faster one-step student preserves the epistemic uncertainty needed for control, rather than only next-state accuracy.
  • Proved that a natural single-latent teacher–student disagreement match does not uniquely identify epistemic uncertainty; constructed counterexamples and derived a multi-latent objective that separately constrains epistemic and aleatoric components.
  • After tracing and fixing a training-pipeline bug, confirmed that the corrected objective recovers both uncertainty channels in controlled and live SAC experiments, and is now being tested for policy-return gains on a pre-registered harder benchmark.

Keyush Nisar, Krishil Parikh, Krisha Maisheri, Aruna Gawade, Nilesh T. Rathod, Angelin Florence A. DIO: Black-Box Online Routing for Heterogeneous vLLM Replicas | The Journal of Supercomputing, Springer | Under review
Manuscript · Code
  • Led the design of a lightweight OpenAI-compatible gateway over stock vLLM. Dual-timescale NLMS learns per-replica service-time slopes from black-box end-to-end telemetry; no engine patches.
  • Achieved 48.3% lower p99 latency than Round-Robin under controlled 2× service-time asymmetry, with ~14µs control-plane overhead.
  • Main transferable finding is negative: a model that ranks replicas well can still over-reject ~40% of healthy traffic if its absolute predictions are used as an SLO gate. Ranking and admission have to be separate policies.
